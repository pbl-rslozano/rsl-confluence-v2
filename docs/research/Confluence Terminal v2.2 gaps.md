# Start the clock, then earn every step

v1.0 2026 · 31 October 2026 · Personal project · Subject: rsL Confluence Terminal v2.2 2026 (build 20261031, engine `6dbcfdc424942e02`)

*Reading convention: a link to a file under `/home/claude/rsl-confluence/` marks a fact checked in the v2.2 source tree as it stood after the post-review fixes on 31 Oct 2026. Web links mark published evidence. **[unverified]** marks claims no primary source in these notes confirmed. Untagged reasoning, effort estimates, dates and gate thresholds are my inferences and design proposals. Nothing here is legal, tax or financial advice.*

v2.2 closes the gap that mattered most in v2.1. The Terminal now records every scored closed bar under a hashed engine id. It measures each outcome from the next bar's open, net of fees, slippage and funding, and its significance statistics no longer pass pure noise half the time. That makes it a working forward-test instrument for the first time, and the single most important action now is to freeze engine `6dbcfdc424942e02` and start a pre-registered 4h crypto clock this week, because every unrecorded month is lost. What is still missing falls into three groups. First, **the decision machinery that turns a ledger into a trustworthy go/no-go is still incomplete.** There is no trials register (the in-app DSR equals the PSR), no PBO, no BTC-excess returns, and only one benchmark, which lives in replay only. Costs are placeholders, the universe is not point-in-time, replay has never been run on real history, and recording still depends on the app being opened. Second, **non-crypto assets are classified but not yet modelled.** Stocks and FX get session clocks, NYSE holidays and an "underlying closed" flag, but the statistics still pool closed-market bars. There are no regular-session bars, no per-class volatility bands, no commodity sessions and no corporate-action or earnings handling. Third, **alerts, Claude and execution are still at zero.** That is correct under the code's own rule that no order route exists until a recorded ledger shows edge net of costs. The recommended order runs as follows: fix the handful of defects that would corrupt the crypto clock (days), then build the G1 decision machinery and a headless recorder while the clock runs, then do multi-asset correctness as a *separately hashed* engine with its own clock. Claude starts at once as an offline ledger analyst. Demo execution waits for G1, and nothing goes live until Saudi access and regulatory status are personally confirmed.

## v2.2 built the instrument the v2.1 roadmap asked for

The v2.1 gap report put measurement ahead of everything, and v2.2 largely delivered its Phase 0 and the core of Phase 1. Each scan writes one ledger row per usable series for its newest closed bar. Rows carry provenance (engine id, app version, source, warm-up bars, rank, 24h volume, a live flag), the four bloc scores, the vote counts, reversal state and, new in v2.2, the asset class and an "underlying closed" flag ([ledger-metrics.ts](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/lib/ledger-metrics.ts), lines 21–63). The engine id is a hash of version, parameters and behaviour. After the code review it fingerprints the whole `scoreSeries` path, including the 1,000-bar window, the FX profile and volume detection, and it is pinned in tests at **`6dbcfdc424942e02`** ([measure.test.mjs](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/lib/measure.test.mjs), line 41; [CHANGELOG.md](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/CHANGELOG.md), line 33). Entry is the next bar's open, exits are at h = 1, 3, 6, 12 and 24 bars, and no outcome is computed across a gap. Since the review, the gap check is exact rather than 5% tolerant ([measurement.md](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/docs/measurement.md)).

The most consequential post-review fix is statistical. The review found that the in-app gate treated heavily overlapping outcomes as independent, so an always-long strategy on a driftless random walk reached PSR ≥ 0.95 in **101 of 200** windows ([v2.2 code review](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/docs/reviews/Confluence-Terminal-v2.2-Code-Review.md), finding #1). The report now uses non-overlapping outcomes per series, one portfolio return per h-bar block for the PSR, and IC cross-sections at least h bars apart (`thinNonOverlapping`, `blockReturns` at [ledger-metrics.ts](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/lib/ledger-metrics.ts), lines 322–408). In the unit test, noise now passes the gate about **3%** of the time. The gate also checks that edge survives a second round trip of fee plus slippage, which is the "2× costs" test ([ledger-dialog.tsx](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/components/ledger-dialog.tsx), lines 66 and 117–131). The dialog pools only the current engine id, source, timeframe and asset class, and it counts rows from other engines without mixing them in (lines 77–81).

The storage and freshness defects that would have corrupted a long clock are fixed too. Symbols whose fetch failed are shown as "feed unavailable" and are neither scored nor recorded ([page.tsx](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/app/page.tsx), lines 582–613). The ledger lives in its own `rsl-conf-ledger` IndexedDB database as one document per bar, and it recovers when storage fills ([signal-ledger.ts](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/lib/signal-ledger.ts), lines 21, 82 and 175). A persisted cursor lets the worker catch up on up to 48 closed bars per series when the app reopens, using full windows only and never the main thread (page.tsx, lines 646–684). Replay windows are gap-aware, CSVs are filtered by interval, and in-sample rows whose longest horizon would reach past the out-of-sample split are embargoed ([replay.cli.mjs](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/lib/replay.cli.mjs), line 159). The test suites stand at **engine 37, measurement 102, FVG 21 and acceptance 64/64**.

## Six gaps still stand between the ledger and a valid verdict

### Recording still depends on the owner opening the app

The catch-up cursor narrows the hole problem but does not remove it. At 48 bars per series, a closed app is covered for **8 days on 4h, 2 days on 1h, 12 hours on 15m** and 48 days on 1d. On the recommended 4h primary test, a desktop install opened at least weekly therefore produces a nearly gap-free series. That substantially weakens the earlier argument that a headless recorder must come first for the *signal* ledger. Three code-level limits remain, and the last two are my reading of the code rather than anything it documents. First, catch-up runs only for series in the current scan's fresh set, so a pair that dropped out of the Top-N or the Watch list is never caught up (page.tsx, lines 653–672). Second, catch-up rows are written with **rank 0 and quote volume 0** (line 667). Every bar recorded while the app was shut therefore has no point-in-time universe data, which makes the stored universe weakest exactly where it matters. Third, the cursor is kept per source × timeframe, not per asset class (lines 652 and 679; [signal-ledger.ts](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/lib/signal-ledger.ts), line 229). Switching the class filter from Crypto to Stocks and back therefore advances one shared cursor. Bars that closed in the class not on screen are never recorded. The fix is to key the cursor by source × timeframe × class, or better, by series.

Durability also needs attention, because the ledger is now explicitly **not part of `.rslbak` backups** ([measurement.md](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/docs/measurement.md), line 11). In the PWA, IndexedDB is best-effort storage that the browser can evict, and the `navigator.storage.persisted()` check is still open (review S11). IndexedDB also has a 400k-row cap. A 100-pair 4h clock writes about 600 rows a day, which fits for roughly two years. A 200-pair 1h clock writes about 4,800 a day and reaches the cap in under three months, after which quota recovery drops the oldest quarter of days. That is my arithmetic from the documented caps. The practical rule follows directly: run the primary clock on the desktop (SQLite) build, keep its scope modest, and export CSV/JSON weekly until a headless recorder writes to a file of its own.

### The gate cannot yet deflate for how many things were tried

The DSR exists, but the dialog calls `buildReport` with `trials = 1` ([ledger-dialog.tsx](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/components/ledger-dialog.tsx), line 100), so its "deflated" figure equals the PSR. That matters more in v2.2 than it did in v2.1, because the app now offers many separate gates: 4 sources × 13 timeframes × 6 class filters × 5 horizons. Each report the owner opens is an implicit trial. Peeking adds a second problem. With a ~3% false-pass rate per look, checking the dialog daily over a 90-day clock and acting on the first pass inflates the real false-positive rate far above 3%, because the looks are correlated but not identical (inference). The Deflated Sharpe Ratio ([SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)) and PBO via combinatorially symmetric cross-validation ([PBO paper](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4w1110bb/qt4w1110bb.pdf)) only work when the trial count comes from a log, not from memory. Two things are needed. A trials register should record every replay configuration and every promoted filter, and feed the DSR in both replay and app. And a pre-registered decision date should replace "pass whenever the badge turns green".

### The target is still raw return, not excess return

The IC is computed on raw forward returns across pairs at each bar. The test the earlier report recommended pre-registering, the 4h rank IC against the 6-bar return *in excess of BTC* for a point-in-time top-30, with a locked holdout, is still not implemented. Neither is a cross-sectional-mean excess return. Without it, a score that merely leans long in a rising market passes the edge test. The only benchmark, a 20-bar momentum sign, exists only in replay ([replay.cli.mjs](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/lib/replay.cli.mjs), lines 110–115). That benchmark carries the most weight: if 18 votes cannot beat one momentum sign, the other 17 add only complexity. Power sets the calendar. With spaced cross-sections at h = 6 on 4h bars, the clock yields about one IC cross-section a day. If the per-cross-section IC has a standard deviation near 1/√30 ≈ 0.18 for 30 roughly independent pairs, t ≥ 2 needs a mean IC of about **0.038 after 90 days and 0.027 after 180 days** (inference). Three months is therefore a floor for anything but a large effect, and six months is the realistic horizon. Serial correlation can overstate an annualised Sharpe by up to 65% ([Lo 2002](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/ufajxx/v58y2002i4p36-52.html)), which is why the block-based statistics are a precondition, not a refinement.

### Costs and universe are still assumptions

Defaults remain spot 10 bp, Bybit linear 5.5 bp, 5 bp placeholder slippage for everything, and a constant 1 bp per 8h funding ([ledger-metrics.ts](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/lib/ledger-metrics.ts), lines 65–80). The 2× check only stresses fee plus slippage, not funding. Those base-tier taker fees match published schedules: 0.05% on Binance perps, 0.055% on Bybit and 0.06% on Bitget ([Binance FAQ](https://www.binance.com/support/faq/360033544231); [Bitsgap](https://bitsgap.com/blog/bybit-trading-fees-explained-what-it-costs); [Bitget support](https://www.bitget.com/support/articles/12560603892733)). Funding is not constant, though. Bybit can change the funding interval per contract dynamically ([Bybit via AAP, Oct 2025](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20251029ae09993)), and weekend funding on TradFi perps is skewed: XAG averaged +56.69% APR on weekends against +18.18% on weekdays on Binance ([BitMEX Q1 2026](https://www.bitmex.com/blog/2026q1-derivatives-report)). Slippage remains the weakest number, and the cheapest fix is still order-book snapshots on the already-allowed `data-api.binance.vision` host. On the universe side, replay uses today's survivors with rank 0. Crypto survivorship bias has been measured at 62% a year for equal-weighted portfolios ([Ammann et al. 2022](https://diffform.netlify.app/publication/2022_liebi_survivorship)), and whether data.binance.vision keeps archives for delisted pairs is still **[unverified]**. A forward-only fix costs almost nothing: log the full, unfiltered ticker ranking once per scan and derive `rk` from that log, for live and catch-up rows alike.

### Smaller defects that still bias the sample

`WARM_MIN_BARS` is still 300 ([ledger-metrics.ts](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/lib/ledger-metrics.ts), line 92). The engine's design window is 1,000 bars, but rows scored on 300–999 bars still enter the statistics. Recursive indicators give "different last-bar values depending on how much history they were seeded with" ([Freqtrade recursive analysis](https://www.freqtrade.io/en/stable/recursive-analysis)), so the threshold for the primary test should be `wb = 1000`. Outcomes are recomputed from a candle cache capped at 10,000 bars and treated as re-fetchable, so a pair that leaves the scan may never get its outcome measured. Storing outcome snapshots once each horizon closes removes that path-dependence. Multi-tab writes stay last-writer-wins, and worker cancellation is open. Neither biases a single-window desktop clock. Finally, the replay has never been run against real exchange history, because the build environment could not reach the exchanges ([README.md](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/README.md), lines 69–78). Until it has, the backtest is tested code with no result.

## Non-crypto assets are classified, but not yet modelled

v2.2 made real progress on asset classes without a new host. Classification uses Bybit's `symbolType` and curated lists. Stocks and FX have DST-aware session clocks. **NYSE holidays and 13:00 ET early closes for 2026–2028** are hard-coded from NYSE's own release ([asset-classes.ts](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/lib/asset-classes.ts), lines 155–200; [NYSE via Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251223981478/en/NYSE-Group-Announces-2026-2027-and-2028-Holiday-and-Early-Closings-Calendar)). Ledger rows carry `uc` for bars printed while the underlying was shut. A banner warns 24 hours before and 2 hours after FOMC, CPI, jobs-report and ECB events, with dates from the [Federal Reserve](https://federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm), the [BLS](https://www.bls.gov/schedule/2026/home.htm) and the [ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/calendars/mgcgc/html/index.en.html) ([macro-calendar.ts](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/src/lib/macro-calendar.ts)). The FX profile also switches off the low-volatility cap. That is enough to *label* a non-crypto signal honestly. It is not enough to *score* one correctly.

The deepest problem is that the measurements still treat a synthetic price as a market price. Off-hours prices are synthetic on every venue. Bybit's TradFi perp index runs on a slow 600-second EWMA when the underlying is closed, with mark price capped at ±5% for single stocks and ±3% for ETFs and commodities ([Bybit Help](https://www.bybit.com/fr-FR/help-center/article/Introduction-to-TradFi-Perpetual-Contracts)). Bybit itself warns that "spreads widen and execution quality may decline" then ([Bybit Learn](https://www.bybit.com/en/learn/read-to-earn/bybit-tradfi-perpetuals)). v2.2 records `uc`, but the report never filters on it, so weekend and overnight drift bars are pooled with real-session bars in the hit rate, edge and IC. Commodities are also treated as always open: `hasSessionGaps` returns true only for stock and FX (asset-classes.ts, lines 153–155). Gold and oil perps therefore never show "underlying closed" over weekends or during the daily 17:00–18:00 ET metals break (break times **[unverified]** from a secondary source: [ForTraders](https://www.fortraders.com/blog/gold-futures-trading-hours)). FX holidays are not modelled at all.

Mature tools set the norm the Terminal should copy. TrendSpider's scanner includes pre- and post-market data only when "Ext Hours" is ticked, which makes regular-session bars the default ([TrendSpider FAQ](https://help.trendspider.com/kb/scanner/market-scanner-faq-markdown-html)). TradingView shows extended-hours data in a separate screener tab ([TradingView Help](https://in.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000502613-how-to-see-the-pre-market-post-market-data-in-the-screener/)), and dividend adjustment is an explicit chart setting, not a default ([TradingView Help](https://www.tradingview.com:443/support/solutions/43000590597-how-can-i-enable-data-adjustment-for-dividends/)). Stock scanners scan named universes. TrendSpider says "you cannot scan every stock at once" and steers users to index and sector lists ([TrendSpider FAQ](https://help.trendspider.com/kb/scanner/market-scanner-faq-markdown-html)).

| Requirement | Mature-tool norm | v2.2 | Gap to close |
|---|---|---|---|
| Session bars | RTH default, extended hours opt-in | Sessions known, all 24/7 bars scored | RTH-only series for stock/ETF perps; `uc = 1` excluded from stats by default |
| Holidays | Exchange calendars built in | NYSE 2026–2028 | FX holidays; metals/energy break and weekend closure; yearly refresh |
| Volatility calibration | Per-symbol normalisation | Global 55/70 bands; FX cap off | ATR/band/squeeze thresholds as percentiles of each symbol's in-session history; separate metals profile |
| Volume semantics | Relative volume | Volume detected per series | Volume votes relative to own median, in-session only; down-weight OBV/CMF/VWAP off-hours |
| Corporate actions | Split-adjusted; dividends opt-in | None | Split detector, cache invalidation, 24h signal mute; multiplier-aware basis |
| Earnings and macro | Screener filter and chart markers | Macro banner only | Macro event as a ledger field; manual earnings list for about 20–30 names |

| Costs | — | One cost per source | Per-class slippage (thin xStocks), per-symbol funding interval |

Corporate actions are the most likely source of silent errors in tokenised stocks. For Bybit stock perps, splits are announced about 7 days ahead and trading is suspended. Prices are divided by the split factor, and "chart prices and historical data may take up to 24 hours to synchronize" ([Bybit Help](https://www.bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Stock-Splits-and-Reverse-Stock-Splits-for-TradFi-Perpetual-Contracts)). Each token type handles dividends differently. xStocks rebase and reinvest dividends ([Kraken](https://support.kraken.com/articles/corporate-actions-xstocks-kraken-app)). Ondo tokens are total-return trackers whose price drifts up by the dividend, and they pause around 19:50–20:10 ET before ex-dates ([Degate](https://degate.com/playbook/tokenized-stock-dividends-mechanisms/)). Bybit's cash-dividend mechanics for stock perps are **[unverified]**, because the article did not render. A rescaled history inside the candle cache, mixed with unadjusted cached bars, would hand the engine a fake crash or spike. A split guard therefore matters more than earnings dates.

One structural constraint shapes the sequencing (inference). Per-class volatility bands, RTH-only bars and new volume rules change the scoring pipeline, and the engine id fingerprints that pipeline. Building them changes the id and starts a new ledger. Two clocks therefore make sense. The crypto clock should start now on `6dbcfdc424942e02`. A non-crypto clock should start only after the multi-asset profile is built and frozen. Rows that today's engine records for stocks and FX are still useful as labelled pilot data, but they should not count toward any G1 verdict for those classes. The first step for the non-crypto clock is still the empirical weekend check the asset-class doc already prescribes ([asset-classes.md](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/docs/asset-classes.md), line 29 onward): find out whether Bybit and Bitget emit missing, flat or drifting bars when the underlying is shut. No exchange documents it.

## Alerts, Claude and execution remain at zero, correctly

Nothing in these three layers moved in v2.2. Alerts are still in-memory, in-app reversal pop-ups. A repo-wide grep finds no Notification API, Telegram, webhook or email code, and the service worker holds only cache logic ([sw.js](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/web/public/sw.js)). There is no Claude, Anthropic, MCP or LLM code anywhere. The gateway still exposes only `/health`, `/profiles`, `POST /connect` and `DELETE /profiles/:id`. Its boundary comment still forbids any order, withdrawal or transfer endpoint "until the signal engine has demonstrated an edge net of costs against a recorded ledger" ([server.mjs](/home/claude/rsl-confluence/apps/confluence/gateway/server.mjs), lines 14–16). The recorded ledger now exists; the demonstrated edge does not. Phases 3–5 therefore stay closed by the code's own rule, and that is the right outcome.

One enabler did arrive. The ledger exports to CSV and JSON, and replay writes `ledger.csv`, `report.json` and `report.md`. Claude can therefore act as an L1 analyst today with no app or network change. It can read each weekly export, split it by engine id and class, compute the statistics the dialog lacks (BTC-excess IC, Wilson intervals, bootstrap, benchmark comparisons), and log every hypothesis as a trial before it is tested. This is the cheapest high-value Claude role. It also checks the in-app gate independently. The next step, a local read-only MCP server over the ledger, fits the Agent SDK, which supports local MCP servers ([Agent SDK MCP](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/mcp)). The Messages API MCP connector accepts only public HTTPS servers ([Claude Platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/mcp-connector)). Community CCXT MCP servers should still be avoided. One widely forked example can place orders at up to 100× leverage, has no read-only mode, and stores keys in plaintext ([raz0r/ccxt-mcp](https://github.com/raz0r/ccxt-mcp)).

Public evidence still argues against letting an LLM trade on its own. In Alpha Arena Season 1 on crypto perps, results ranged from +22% to about −63% over two weeks ([GNcrypto](https://www.gncrypto.news/news/qwen-wins-alpha-arena-season-1-with-22-percent-returns/); [ForkLog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/)). In Season 1.5 on US equities, seven of eight models lost money ([Aihola](https://aihola.com/article/grok-wins-alpha-arena-trading)). These are short, secondary-sourced windows. They do not measure skill, but they do show that no unconstrained LLM trader has demonstrated an edge. The exchange precedent points the same way. Binance's MCP server works inside a dedicated agentic sub-account, has no withdrawal scope, and requires user confirmation before any order ([Binance Developers](https://developers.binance.com/en/docs/agent-native/mcp-server/agentic)). The design rule from v2.1 therefore stands. Every order, whoever proposed it, passes a deterministic risk engine in the gateway: sizing to lot filters, structural stops, open-risk and correlated-cluster caps, daily and weekly loss locks, a kill switch and a dead-man's switch. Testing then runs on exchange demo accounts. Bybit demo keeps orders only 7 days ([Bybit Demo](https://bybit-exchange.github.io/docs/v5/demo)), and Binance demo data is "not real market data" ([Binance Demo Mode](https://developers.binance.com/docs/binance-spot-api-docs/demo-mode/general-info)), so the Terminal must own its journal from the first paper trade.

Multi-asset support raises the stakes on access. Saudi Arabia does not appear on Bybit's restricted-country list as updated 16 Oct 2026 ([Bybit](https://bybit.com/en/help-center/article/Service-Restricted-Countries)). Bybit's TradFi perps carry separate product-level eligibility, are excluded for EEA residents, and are issued through a Mauritius-licensed entity ([AAP, May 2026](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20260508ae54873/)). Bitget says stock-perp "availability may vary by region" ([Bitget Academy](https://www.bitget.com/academy/trade-stock-perps-with-100x-leverage-on-bitget-2026-guide)). Binance bStocks and TradFi perps are limited to eligible jurisdictions. **Whether a Saudi resident may trade these specific products is [unverified]**, and no 2025–2026 primary SAMA or CMA statement on offshore derivatives, CFDs or tokenised equities was found. The official record found is the 2018 statement that virtual currencies are unlicensed ([Baker McKenzie](https://blockchain.bakermckenzie.com/2018/08/15/unauthorized-cryptocurrency-declared-illegal-in-saudi-arabia/)). Treat that as factual context, not legal advice. A read-only scanner of public data needs no account. Before any order, confirm eligibility directly with each exchange and the current position with SAMA, CMA or Saudi counsel. The software should degrade to spot-only or analysis-only if access changes, and it must never help route around a restriction.

## A re-prioritised roadmap with seven gated phases

The principle does not change: nothing that risks money ships before the measurement that could justify it. The order changes in three ways. First, the 48-bar catch-up makes a desktop-run 4h clock viable now, so the clock starts before the headless recorder exists. The calendar follows from the clock. A crypto clock started in the first week of November 2026 reaches its three-month floor in **early February 2027** and its six-month mark in **May 2027**. Phases B and C fit inside that window, so the decision machinery will be ready before there is enough data to use it. A non-crypto clock cannot start until Phase D freezes its engine, which puts its earliest verdict around mid-2027. Holiday and macro tables need refreshing by then: the BLS schedule is published only through December 2026, and the FOMC 2027 dates are tentative.

The no-go branches are as important as the go branches. If G1 fails because the score cannot beat a single momentum sign, simplify rather than enrich. Collapse the nine-member trend bloc, reconsider the contrarian oscillators on slow bars, then ship a new hashed engine and restart the clock. If it fails twice, the Terminal remains a measured analysis tool with Claude as its analyst. That is a legitimate product, and its read-only, credential-free design is already its strongest property. If non-crypto classes show edge only on bars where `uc = 1`, the signal is measuring the venue's smoothing mechanism, not the underlying market, and those classes should stay display-only.

## Conclusion

v2.2 changes what the bottleneck is. In v2.1 the Terminal could not produce evidence. Now it can, and the binding constraints are calendar time and the owner's discipline. That makes the most valuable work in the next month mostly non-code: a dated pre-registration, a fixed decision date, a trials log, and a weekly export that Claude audits. Peeking at a green badge is now the main way to fool yourself, because the statistics underneath it are finally sound enough to be believed.

The multi-asset extension carries a less obvious lesson. Classification made stocks, FX and gold visible in a crypto scanner. But on these venues, a large share of the bars a 24/7 venue prints come from smoothing formulas while the real market is shut. A scanner that scores those bars as if they were trades will discover "edge" in Bybit's EWMA rather than in Nvidia or the euro. For non-crypto assets, the key question is therefore which bars count, not which indicator to add. Answering it before the second clock starts is what keeps the ledger honest.

## Addendum — fixed after this report (31 Oct 2026, same build)

The three code problems flagged above were fixed before release and are covered by tests:
- **Catch-up cursor** is now kept per source × timeframe × **asset-class view** (`signal-ledger.ts` `cursor(src, tf, cls)`), so switching the class filter no longer skips another class's bars.

- **Commodities** now follow the CME Globex session (Sunday 18:00 → Friday 17:00 New York, daily 17:00–18:00 break), so gold/oil perps and tokenised gold get the "underlying closed" flag. Commodity holidays are still not modelled.
